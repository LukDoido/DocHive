When companies call us

If one of these is on your desk this quarter.

Nobody wakes up wanting to improve their filing. Something forces the question, and it is usually one of these four.

A due diligence

A buyer or a bank asks for every supplier agreement with a term over one year. Assembling that list by hand is the moment most people decide something has to change.

An audit

Two years of documents, requested in a week, spread across a drive, three mailboxes and someone's desktop. The work is finding them, not producing them.

A renewal that slipped

The contract rolled over for another year because the notice period ran out quietly. Usually the cost of that one miss is larger than a year of DocHive.

A key person leaving

The filing logic lived in one head, and that head is going. Rules written down and applied consistently are the difference between a handover and an excavation.

If none of these apply, there is a fair chance you do not need this yet. We will say so on the call.

The fair question

Why not just SharePoint?

Because storing is a different job from reading. SharePoint keeps what you put in it, under the name you gave it, in the folder you chose. It does not know the notice period is three months, and it will not tell you in September.

Metadata columns can carry that, if someone fills them in on every document, for years. That is the part that stops happening around month four.

DocHive does not replace it. SharePoint stays where it is. DocHive reads the new document, proposes the name, moves it through the official API and watches the date. A good structure in SharePoint is an advantage, there is something to file into.
